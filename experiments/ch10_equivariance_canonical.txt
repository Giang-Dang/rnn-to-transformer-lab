1. one convolution, 3x3, shifted by one pixel

padding     max |f(shift x) - shift f(x)|
circular    0.000e+00

and for zero padding, where that error sits:
  whole feature map          1.772e+00
  interior, borders dropped  0.000e+00

the interior is bit-exact. zero padding does not weaken
equivariance, it breaks it on a two-pixel frame and nowhere else.

2. subsampling: conv then MaxPool2d(2)
   an input shift of 2 should move the output by 1, so that is
   what the output is compared against

input shift  output shift  error
0            0             0.000e+00
2            1             0.000e+00
4            2             0.000e+00
6            3             0.000e+00
8            4             0.000e+00

exact at every even shift. now the odd ones, which have no output
shift to be compared against - so the residual below is the best
any output shift achieves, searched over all of them:

input shift  best output shift  residual  residual/scale
1            -2                 2.327e+00 0.9439
3            -3                 2.258e+00 0.9159
5            -2                 2.258e+00 0.9159

output magnitude 2.4651, so the best any output shift can
do for an odd input shift is to miss by more than nine tenths of
that scale. this is not a degradation that grows with distance -
the layer has nothing at all to say about half of the shifts.

2b. it is the subsampling, not the max
    Zhang 2019 is usually read as being about max pooling and
    aliasing. the same test on three ways of halving the grid:

layer           even shift  odd shift  scale   odd/scale
MaxPool2d(2)    0.000e+00   2.215e+00  2.429   0.912
AvgPool2d(2)    0.000e+00   1.101e+00  1.343   0.820
Conv stride 2   0.000e+00   2.968e+00  2.427   1.223

all three are bit-exact on even shifts and all three are wrong by
the order of the signal on odd ones. there is no max operation in
the third row at all. so the even/odd split is a property of
throwing away every other sample, not of the nonlinearity that
happens to sit next to it.

3. depth multiplies the period

pool layers  total stride  exact at shifts of
1            2             2
2            4             4
3            8             8

a three-pool network on a 32 pixel image is exact at multiples
of 8, which is 4 of the 32 horizontal shifts, or one in eight.
LeCun et al. 1989 said this in words about their own two-pixel
subsampling: the input image is undersampled and some position
information is eliminated.
